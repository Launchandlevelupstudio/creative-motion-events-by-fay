Meet Fayola

Creative direction & event design
Her Approach

Fayola Whyte-Manco is a Washington DC event designer and creative director working across DC, Maryland and Virginia. She designs the room and directs the production herself: concept, installations, tablescapes, vendor direction, install and breakdown, so the people in it can simply be present.

Since 2017 she's produced more than 200 events, nearly all of them by referral: association galas and awards nights, a bank branch grand opening, an embassy cultural celebration, and the milestone birthdays, showers and dinners families remember for years.

Her Story

Long before the business, there was a little girl pulling her friends together, arranging the food, setting the space, making sure everyone felt special. Not because anyone asked; because making people feel good has always been who she is. Fayola designed her own wedding in 2011, turned that passion into a company in 2017, and still puts her heart into every room herself. The florals, the installations, the details are executed with precision. What drives her is people.

"Life moves fast and quietly. We get so caught up in the everyday that we forget to stop, to celebrate ourselves, to celebrate each other."

What Does a $15,000 Gala Design Budget Actually Buy in Washington DC?

A line-by-line answer for association boards, nonprofit committees, and corporate planners pricing out an annual gala in DC, Maryland, or Virginia.

Short answer: in the Washington DC market, a $15,000 design and production budget typically covers full creative direction, a branded stage or step-and-repeat, tablescapes and centerpieces for 150–250 guests, entry and registration styling, signage, vendor coordination, and a install-and-breakdown crew, with the designer's fee shown separately from hard costs. Here's exactly where that money goes.

Design fee vs. production cost: the split that matters

Every reputable proposal separates two line items: the design fee (creative direction, concept development, vendor sourcing, project management, and on-site oversight) and production cost (the actual florals, rentals, linens, signage, and labor). On a $15,000 gala, the design fee typically runs 20–30% of the total, with the remainder going to physical production. If a proposal doesn't separate these, it's worth asking why. It usually means the designer's time is being buried inside marked-up rentals instead of billed transparently.

A sample $15,000 breakdown

  • Creative direction & design fee~25%
  • Branded stage, step-and-repeat & entry moment~20%
  • Tablescapes, centerpieces & linens~20%
  • Florals & installations~15%
  • Signage & custom printed material~8%
  • Install, breakdown & on-site labor~12%

This is a starting template, not a fixed formula. A committee that already owns linens or is renting a pre-decorated ballroom will shift more of the budget toward florals and branding; an embassy or cultural event may shift more toward protocol-aware installations and less toward signage.

What pushes the number up

Guest count above 250, a venue with load-in restrictions or union labor requirements, a rush timeline under six weeks, custom fabrication (marquee letters, oversized backdrops, branded floor decals), and multiple room transformations in one night (cocktail hour to dinner to dance floor) all add cost. So does asking for florals that are out of season or imported.

What keeps it at $15,000 instead of $25,000

Reusing a strong existing brand palette instead of a full rebrand each year, booking 90 days out instead of 30, choosing a venue with in-house tables, chairs and linens, and committing to an Annual Design Partnership so the designer isn't rebuilding a concept from zero every year. All of this keeps a gala at the same quality without the price climbing annually.

Corporate and nonprofit budgets aren't priced the same way

A corporate-sponsored gala usually has more flexibility on production value because sponsor dollars are funding visibility. The branded stage and step-and-repeat need to photograph well for sponsors' own marketing. A nonprofit or association gala more often needs the budget to stretch across both design and a silent auction or program element, so the design fee conversation happens earlier and the production choices lean more efficient by design, not by looking cheaper.

How to get an accurate number for your event

The honest answer is that no designer can price a gala from a guest count alone. The number moves on venue, timeline, guest count, and how much of the room needs to be built versus enhanced. The fastest way to get a real number is a short consultation: fifteen minutes on the event, the venue, and what "success" looks like for your sponsors or board, followed by a proposal that shows the design fee and production cost as two separate lines, exactly as described above.
